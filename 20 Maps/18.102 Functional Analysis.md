# 18.102 Functional Analysis

> Parent: [[Home]] · Reference map of [[Kehle - 18.102 Functional Analysis Lecture Notes (2025)|Kehle's Spring 2025 lecture notes]] — built for **looking things up and remembering why they matter**, not for first learning.
>
> The course in one sentence: **linear algebra in infinite dimensions, where nothing is automatic** — subspaces needn't be closed, linear maps needn't be continuous, unit balls aren't compact — and the entire subject is the toolkit (completeness, duality, compactness substitutes) that wins those properties back.

## 📖 The notes, chapter by chapter

- [[Functional Analysis Definitions]] — 🔑 **the glossary**: every load-bearing definition of the course on one page, grouped by chapter
- [[Topological and Metric Spaces]] — *ch. 1, the substrate*: Zorn, topologies, metric completeness, and the Baire category theorem (the engine behind the ch. 2 cornerstones)
- [[Normed and Banach Spaces]] — *ch. 2, the home objects*: bounded = continuous, Hahn–Banach (duals are rich), and the three Baire cornerstones: uniform boundedness, open mapping, closed graph
- [[Measure Theory and the Lebesgue Integral]] — *ch. 3, the integral that closes*: Carathéodory's machine, Lebesgue/Hausdorff measure, the three convergence theorems, $L^p$ complete (Riesz–Fischer)
- [[Dual Spaces and Weak Topologies]] — *ch. 4, compactness regained*: weak and weak-* topologies, Banach–Alaoglu, $(L^p)' = L^q$
- [[Hilbert Spaces]] — *ch. 5, geometry restored*: projection onto closed convex sets, Riesz representation (self-dual), orthonormal bases and Parseval, adjoints
- [[Spectral Theory]] — *ch. 6, the payoff*: spectrum replaces eigenvalues, compact operators, the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators, Fredholm alternative

## 🧭 How the chapters depend on each other

Baire (ch. 1) powers the cornerstones (ch. 2) → measure theory (ch. 3) builds the main *examples* $L^p$ → duality (ch. 4) recovers compactness where the unit ball fails → Hilbert space (ch. 5) is the $p=2$ special case where everything is best → spectral theory (ch. 6) cashes it all in to diagonalize operators and solve equations.
