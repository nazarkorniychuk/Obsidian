# Measure Theory and the Lebesgue Integral

> **Where this sits.** Chapter 3 of [[18.102 Functional Analysis]] — the longest chapter, and the construction of the course's main *examples*: the $L^p$ spaces. Three reasons Riemann isn't enough, and each names a payoff below: **(1)** integrate over general sets, not just intervals ($1_{\mathbb{Q}}$ becomes integrable); **(2)** completeness — Riemann-integrable functions under $\int|f-g|$ are *not* complete, and $L^1$ is exactly the missing completion; **(3)** convergence theorems — Riemann only tolerates uniform limits, Lebesgue tolerates pointwise-a.e. + domination. (Also: the foundation of modern probability.)

## Measure spaces — the axioms of "volume"

- **σ-algebra** $\mathcal{F}$: contains $\emptyset$, closed under complement and *countable* union. **Measure** $\mu: \mathcal{F} \to [0,\infty]$: $\mu(\emptyset)=0$ + countable additivity on disjoint sets. **Null set**: measure zero; "**almost everywhere**" = off a null set — the resolution at which all of $L^p$ theory operates
- **Continuity of measure**: increasing unions and decreasing intersections (one finite) pass to limits — countable additivity's workhorse form
- Generated σ-algebra: smallest one containing a family (intersection of all); **Borel** = generated by the open sets

## Carathéodory's machine — why not all sets are measurable

**Vitali's impossibility**: no map on *all* subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ can be countably additive, translation-invariant, and give intervals their length. Something must be given up — and the right answer is *universality*: restrict to a σ-algebra of "measurable" sets.

The construction: an **outer measure** $\mu^*$ (monotone, countably *sub*additive, defined on everything) → **Carathéodory's criterion** picks out the good sets: $A$ is measurable iff it *slices every set additively*, $\mu^*(B) = \mu^*(B \cap A) + \mu^*(B \setminus A)\ \forall B$ → **Extension theorem**: these sets form a σ-algebra on which $\mu^*$ is a genuine, *complete* measure (subsets of null sets are null).

**Lebesgue measure**: $\lambda^*(A) = \inf \sum \operatorname{vol}(C_i)$ over countable open-cuboid covers, run through the machine. Facts: agrees with volume on cuboids; translation invariant; all open/closed sets measurable; **regular** (inner by compacts, outer by opens); Lebesgue σ-algebra = completion of Borel. Non-measurable sets exist but *only via the axiom of choice* — rule of thumb: any set you can write down is measurable. (Banach–Tarski is the choice-powered pathology made vivid.)

**Hausdorff measure** $\mathcal{H}_s$: cover by arbitrary sets of diameter ≤ δ, weight by $d(U_i)^s$, let δ→0. For each set there's a critical $s_0$ — the **Hausdorff dimension** — below which $\mathcal{H}_s = \infty$, above which $0$. The tool for fractals; $\mathcal{H}_n \sim \lambda$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$.

## Measurable functions — the stability payoff

$f$ **measurable** iff preimages of Borel sets are measurable (test just $\{f > \alpha\}$). Continuous ⇒ measurable. The property Riemann never had: measurability survives **everything** — sums, products, $\min/\max$, and crucially $\sup_n, \inf_n, \limsup, \liminf$, so **pointwise a.e. limits of measurable functions are measurable**.

**The fundamental approximation**: every non-negative measurable $f$ is the *increasing* pointwise limit of simple functions (finitely-valued: $\sum \lambda_i 1_{A_i}$) — chop the *codomain* into dyadic layers $\{j 2^{-n} \le f < (j{+}1)2^{-n}\}$. This is the Riemann/Lebesgue picture in one line: **Riemann partitions the domain, Lebesgue partitions the range** — which is why wild domains are fine as long as level sets are measurable.

## The integral, in three stages

1. Simple: $\int \sum \lambda_i 1_{A_i} = \sum \lambda_i \mu(A_i)$
2. Non-negative measurable: $\int f = \lim \int f_n$ along any increasing simple approximation (well-defined)
3. Signed/complex: $f$ **integrable** iff $\int |f| < \infty$; then $\int f = \int f^+ - \int f^-$

**The three convergence theorems** — the whole reason for the construction:

| theorem | hypotheses | conclusion | mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monotone convergence** | $0 \le f_n \uparrow f$ | $\int f_n \to \int f$ | continuity of measure |
| **Fatou** | $f_n \ge 0$ | $\int \liminf f_n \le \liminf \int f_n$ | MCT on $\inf_{k \ge n} f_k$; inequality can be strict — mass can escape |
| **Dominated convergence** | $f_n \to f$ a.e., $|f_n| \le g$ integrable | $\int |f_n - f| \to 0$ | Fatou applied to $2g - |f_n - f|$ |

Relation to Riemann: every Riemann-integrable function is Lebesgue-integrable with the same value; bounded $f$ is Riemann-integrable **iff its discontinuity set is null**. Edge case: $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ on $[0,\infty)$ is *improperly* Riemann but not Lebesgue integrable ($\int|f| = \infty$). Bonus DCT application: FTC holds for any differentiable $f$ with *bounded* derivative — no continuity of $f'$ needed. **Fubini** (stated, not proved): for $f \ge 0$ or integrable, double integrals split into iterated ones in either order.

## $L^p$ spaces — the payoff for functional analysis

$L^p(\Omega)$ = measurable functions with $\int |f|^p < \infty$, **modulo equality a.e.** (else $\|\cdot\|_p$ is only a seminorm); $L^\infty$ uses the essential sup. Hölder and Minkowski transfer verbatim from [[Topological and Metric Spaces|the $\ell^p$ versions]].

- **Riesz–Fischer: $L^p$ is complete**, $1 \le p \le \infty$ — reason (2) delivered; these are Banach spaces. *Mechanism:* pick a fast Cauchy subsequence ($2^{-k}$ gaps), dominate the telescoping series, MCT + DCT finish. Byproduct worth remembering: **$L^p$-convergence ⇒ some subsequence converges pointwise a.e.** — the full sequence needn't (the "typewriter" sequence converges in $L^1$ but pointwise nowhere)
- On *finite* measure spaces: $L^p \subset L^q$ for $q \le p$ (bigger exponent = smaller space), dense inclusions. $L^p(\mathbb{R}^n)$ separable for $p < \infty$, with $C_c^\infty$ dense — the approximation backbone of PDE theory
- **Uniform convexity** ($1 < p < \infty$, via Clarkson's inequalities): the midpoint of two distant unit vectors lies well inside the ball. Buys: reflexivity (**Milman–Pettis**), and the direct minimization proof of [[Dual Spaces and Weak Topologies|$(L^p)^* = L^q$]]. Fails for $p = 1, \infty$ — and indeed those are the non-reflexive ones
- $\ell^p$ is the special case: counting measure on $\mathbb{N}$
